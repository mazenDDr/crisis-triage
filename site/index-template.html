Crisis triage board

During a disaster, thousands of messages arrive at once. A model can sort many of them, but only the answers it is truly sure about should skip a person. Pick a system and a rule, and watch real test tweets get sorted: confident answers go straight through, the rest wait for a person.

Every answer here was recorded on held-out test data (disasters that came after everything used for tuning); nothing runs in your browser. Numbers come from crisis-triage.

How this was measured

  • Every system answers the same 8-class question ("which kind of information does this crisis message give?"). The class set and question wording were chosen on dev data.
  • "Aim for 95%" picks, on dev data only, the lowest confidence cut at which the kept answers are at least 95% right, then applies that cut to the test tweets. The accuracy shown is what the test tweets actually got. "Trust the model" sends an answer whenever the model itself says it is at least 95% sure.
  • "No safe cut" means that on dev data even the most confident answers were not right often enough, so nothing would be sent automatically.
  • Fine-tuned Laya and e5 + LR were trained on past disasters only; the LLMs and zero-shot Laya saw no labels. Full method, intervals and code: github.com/mazenDDr/crisis-triage. The fine-tuned model: mazenDDr/laya-crisis-triage.
